Presentation at the 40th Interamerican Congress of Psychology

Created on July 14, 2025

2025   ·   congress   C-PTSD

This past July, I had the honor of participating in the 40th Interamerican Congress of Psychology (40CIP), held in the vibrant city of San Juan, Puerto Rico. Under the theme “A Psychology for all the Americas,” this event not only marked the Congress’s return to the “Island of Enchantment” after three decades, but also served as a fundamental epicenter for interdisciplinary exchange across our region.

Project Overview: Neurocomputational Profiling of C-PTSD

The project aims to move beyond symptomatic diagnosis by leveraging the Research Domain Criteria (RDoC) framework, shifting the focus toward biological and behavioral mechanisms. By integrating computational modeling with traditional clinical measures, the team seeks to identify the “signatures” of trauma in women exposed to repetitive violence.

Key Components of the Research

Clinical Framework: The study distinguishes C-PTSD from traditional PTSD, focusing on the pervasive effects of chronic, repetitive trauma—such as domestic violence—on self-concept and affective regulation.

Methodological Innovation: The research employs a tripartite strategy:

Computational Modeling (Two-Bandit Task): Utilizes Reinforcement Learning algorithms to analyze decision-making processes under uncertainty.

Electrophysiology (Auditory Oddball Task): Uses ERPs (Event-Related Potentials) to capture neural responses to sensory stimuli.

Phenomenological Assessment (3D Body Image Scale): Explores the somatic and self-perception dimensions of trauma.
